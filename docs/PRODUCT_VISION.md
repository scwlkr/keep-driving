# Keep Driving Product Vision

Status: resolved product-identity baseline; numeric content targets remain provisional until the production-throughput gate
Vision date: 2026-07-11

## Product summary

**Keep Driving** is the internal working title for a premium, offline-first, single-player, top-down survival-driving roguelite where **every road is a build decision**. The player takes a battered car from a refuge Garage into a broken open county, reads the road ahead, chooses safer or faster routes, completes risky expeditions, recovers parts, and decides when to return before Fuel, Vehicle Integrity, weather, or threats turn a good haul into a loss.

The product vision is title-independent and release planning assumes a rename. A released road-trip game already uses `Keep Driving`, overlaps in route choice and car customization, and asserts trademark rights. Retain the exact name only if qualified written legal and positioning review affirmatively clears it; otherwise clear a distinctive replacement before any production App ID, store record, logo, icon, public URL, marketing, or branded final-art lock.

The 1.0 world is a connected road atlas built from authored regions, road hierarchy, shortcuts, landmarks, and safehouses. Damaged crossings and restoration milestones gate access diegetically; there are no invisible level walls, and restored connections remain freely explorable. Deterministic systems remix hazards, route closures, weather, pickups, and encounters so the player learns a real place without receiving the same expedition twice. The car is both movement and weapon; there is no on-foot mode or unrelated combat layer. Zombies are one supporting encounter set, not the headline promise or the only source of pressure.

The primary game gains destinations, voluntary extraction, authored progression, recognizable places, build specialization, and a real completion arc. If retained by D020, the prototype's endless survival run returns as **Expedition Mode after primary completion**, not as a partial late-campaign unlock.

Here, *roguelite* means expedition conditions reset and unsecured haul remains at risk while the profile, restored county, discoveries, chassis, parts, and completed campaign milestones persist. The primary profile never wipes and failure never restarts the campaign.

## Core fantasy

> I turn a wreck into *my* route-solving machine, restore a broken county, take one more calculated risk, and bring both the car and the haul home.

The fantasy combines freedom, mechanical expression, survival pressure, and mastery of a changing but learnable world. The desired emotional rhythm is:

1. Anticipation while selecting a route and loadout.
2. Flow while the car feels responsive and the road opens up.
3. Tension when Fuel, cargo, damage, weather, or Pursuit Pressure narrows the options.
4. Improvisation when a shortcut, roadblock, or encounter changes the plan.
5. Relief and pride on a successful return.
6. Curiosity about the next route, part, landmark, or build.

## Player role

The player is a route runner based at the last working Garage in a broken county. They reconnect safehouses, restore a usable road network, recover vehicles and parts, and keep essential movement alive. The role is expressed through driving and mechanical choices rather than dialogue-heavy heroics.

## Target player

Primary audience:

- Players who enjoy readable arcade driving, route planning, roguelite decisions, buildcraft, and exploration.
- Players who want 15-25 minute sessions that can chain into a longer evening.
- Players who prefer a premium, offline-capable game without ads, energy, loot boxes, or mandatory accounts.
- iPhone players who expect console-like clarity and feel from a native landscape App Store game, with touch and controller support.

Desktop keyboard/controller builds are mandatory development, playtest, and quality references; a desktop retail SKU is not committed for 1.0. The game is not aimed at simulation purists, twitch-only racing players, idle progression players, party-management road-trip RPG players, or players looking for an on-foot open-world RPG.

## Unique selling proposition

> Build the car. Read the county. Take the risk. Bring it home.

The differentiated product promise is an illustrated open-county driving-survival game where every installed part changes which routes, shortcuts, cargo, and hazards the car can handle—and every restored connection changes the place the player is learning.

Keep Driving should be recognizable within seconds through:

- A bold animated-road-atlas view with inked top-down cars and terrain.
- A battered yellow hero car against charcoal roads, sun-faded land, cyan route/water accents, and crimson threats.
- Roads that are advantages, not rails: the player can leave them anywhere.
- Safehouses, crossings, and shortcuts that visibly change as the county is restored.
- A compact cockpit-like HUD built around Fuel, Integrity, cargo, and route pressure.
- Weighty but accessible Arcade-Real Handling, visible drift, surface response, and satisfying ram impacts.
- The recurring decision: continue toward a better reward, divert through risk, or return with what is already secured.

### Positioning boundaries

- Not YCJY's road-trip management RPG: no party, hitchhikers, turn-based challenge system, cross-country journey, or disposable route map—and the public name is assumed to change.
- Not *Pacific Drive* from above: no first-person repair loop, supernatural investigation, or Garage-plus-scavenging pitch as the main differentiator; route topology, direct handling, and county restoration lead.
- Not a rally game: there are no laps, championships, pace notes, or time-trial ladder at the center; handling serves navigation, survival, and extraction.
- Not a zombie-driving game with route dressing: threats support the road/build/return loop and can change fiction without changing the product identity.

## Design pillars

### 1. Road choice is the game

Every useful stretch should present information and consequence. Fast pavement attracts threats and closures. Offroad cuts save distance but cost Fuel, grip, cargo stability, or damage. Shortcuts depend on the build. The map is not scenery around combat; navigation is the central verb.

Feature test: if a feature does not create, reveal, or reward a driving/route decision, it needs a strong reason to exist.

### 2. The car is the character

The player expresses intent through chassis and parts. An armored rammer, quiet scout, cargo hauler, offroad explorer, and agile drifter should prefer different roads and solve encounters differently. Upgrades are not a longer stat bar; each part changes capability and carries a cost.

Feature test: a meaningful part must alter how the player drives, routes, risks, or recovers—not merely add an invisible percentage.

### 3. The return makes the risk meaningful

Recovered cargo and uninstalled parts become safe when extracted at a Garage or safehouse. Completion, voluntary return, and failure produce distinct outcomes. The player should frequently know that going farther may be worth it and may also be a mistake.

Feature test: rewards must create a real continue/return decision without erasing all progress on failure.

### 4. Readable at speed

Terrain, hazards, objectives, threats, pickups, damage, and feedback must be understood in peripheral vision on a phone. The game communicates through silhouette, value, motion, sound, haptics, and concise language; color is never the only signal.

Feature test: a new player at the target mobile viewport can identify the next action and danger without reading debug text.

### 5. Crafted places, systemic replay

The player learns authored regions, landmarks, road hierarchy, and shortcuts. Deterministic encounters, weather, closures, contracts, and rewards recombine around those places. Pure random infinity is not a substitute for memorable exploration; one-time authored content is not enough for replayability.

Feature test: repeated travel should strengthen world knowledge while still asking a different tactical question.

## Ethical product principles

- Launch as a paid app containing the complete 1.0 game. Final regional price is validated near launch; the cited same-platform precedent proves only that a premium iPhone driving product exists, not what this game can charge.
- No ads, energy timers, loot boxes, pay-to-win parts, consumable real-money currency, streak punishment, or forced account.
- No gameplay-critical daily obligation. A seeded challenge may be added only if it remains playable offline and does not punish absence.
- Clear costs, effects, tradeoffs, failure consequences, and save behavior.
- Respect pause, interruption, accessibility, and short-session exits.
- No advertising or cross-app tracking. No third-party analytics SDK at launch. Privacy-minimal crash diagnostics may be added only with a specific support benefit, data inventory, disclosure, retention limit, platform/privacy review, and opt-in where required; declining diagnostics never changes gameplay.

Alternatives rejected for launch:

- Free with ads trades clarity and privacy for an incentive to prolong sessions.
- Free-to-play progression, cosmetic IAP, or a full-game unlock makes StoreKit/live-operations work part of an unproven launch and weakens the complete-game promise.
- Subscription has no continuous service that justifies recurring payment.
- Paid plus expansion becomes paymium; reconsider only after 1.0 is complete and stable, for a substantial non-consumable expansion that does not remove promised base content.

## Target 1.0 experience

### Launch content target

The following counts are a coherent **provisional production target**, not a source-proven minimum. Identity, a complete campaign arc, route/build diversity, presentation, accessibility, reliability, and App Store quality are hard requirements. Raw counts lock only after the representative production tranche below proves authoring throughput and decision density.

- One seamless county with **three connected regions**, each with a distinct route, terrain, threat, weather, and visual identity.
- At least **15 major landmarks** and **six safehouses/return points**, with authored road loops and discoverable shortcuts.
- At least **24 authored contract objectives** distributed across four activity families, plus **15 or more dynamic encounter templates**.
- **Three vehicle chassis** with visibly different mass, cargo, handling, durability, and slot profiles.
- **Six functional part slots** and at least **36 launch parts**, all catalog-driven and sidegrade-oriented; at least five viable build archetypes.
- One compact currency, Scrap; recovered parts and blueprints are equipment/unlocks rather than extra spendable currencies.
- A primary restoration arc targeting **8-12 hours** for a first completion, with **20-30 hours** of world completion, builds, mastery trials, and Expedition Mode if retained by D020.
- Day/night presentation and limited region-aware weather that change visibility, grip, route pressure, and ambience without becoming survival-meter clutter.
- Full start/pause/results/map/Garage/loadout/settings/accessibility/save/error flows.
- Production art, animation, audio, VFX, haptics, readable touch controls, controller support, reliable saves, and a tested iOS release.

### Scope-lock gate

Phase 3 must publish `docs/production/SCOPE_LOCK.md`; no world/content/catalog multiplication begins without its PASS. The artifact needs:

1. A dated target candidate and launch window; available internal person-hours by discipline through that window after leave/overhead; committed contractor hours/cash; device, tester, localization, legal, and release budgets. An indefinitely movable date is not capacity.
2. Repeatable production evidence from one Lowlands tranche: one production landmark cluster, one safehouse state change, three objective variants across at least two families, five encounter/event variants, and twelve production-quality parts with final-pipeline art/audio/localization metadata. Record hours by discipline, rework, defects, package/memory cost, and player decision density.
3. Either a representative sample or an explicit three-point estimate for every unique/fixed tranche: another region kit and transition, alternate chassis, weather/night stack, finale, Expedition Mode, mobile adaptation/device matrix, App Store assets/compliance, support, and launch. Any low-confidence item consuming more than 10% of a discipline's remaining capacity requires a prototype before PASS.
4. Mandatory integration, QA, accessibility, localization, legal, device, App Review, and release work costed as baseline—not hidden inside contingency—then a separate 30% unallocated risk reserve.

For every discipline `d`:

```text
required_d = repeatable_content_d + unique_fixed_d + integration_d + QA_d
             + accessibility_d + localization_d + platform_release_d
budgeted_d = required_d * 1.30
PASS_d = budgeted_d <= confirmed_remaining_capacity_d
```

The scope passes only if every discipline passes, total cash/contractor/device/tester budgets pass, the critical path fits the dated window, content-density/repetition/build-diversity tests pass, and no P0/P1 evidence gap is being treated as zero cost.

The **minimum coherent fallback**, not the target, is two full region identities plus a compact mechanically distinct finale route; at least eight landmarks and three safehouses; three route-centered activity families; 12 authored objectives and eight encounter templates; two chassis, five functional slots, 24 named parts, and four viable route-changing builds; a 5-8 active-hour ending and 10-15 active hours of no-filler completion/mastery. The ending, regional contrast, route/build/return loop, decision density, build diversity, accessibility, save integrity, supported-device quality, and App Store readiness remain invariant.

If the provisional target fails, cut Required work in this order:

1. Remove separate post-campaign Expedition Mode; retain campaign replay and mastery.
2. Remove restored-route fast travel and the cosmetic floor.
3. Reduce the full day/night and weather multiplicity to the smallest readable route-condition set.
4. Fold route mastery into the other activities instead of retaining it as a separate fourth family.
5. Remove the third chassis.
6. Compress the third full region into a compact mechanically distinct finale.
7. Reduce safehouses from six toward the three-safehouse fallback while preserving a coherent return network.
8. Merge the sixth functional slot into the remaining five-slot model without hiding part tradeoffs.
9. Reduce the five-build target toward four viable route-changing builds; build diversity may not fall below four.
10. Reduce objective, encounter, part, and landmark quantity—but not below the coherent fallback—while its density, repetition, and build-diversity tests still pass.

Playtime targets rebaseline from the accepted content; enjoyment, ending, no-grind, decision-density, and no-filler gates do not. Each cut triggers the affected validation again. If the fallback also fails, the scope gate is FAIL and the project must change capacity, window, or product decision before production scale-up.

### Current provisional three-region shape

1. **The Lowlands** — readable roads, farms, flooded crossings, tutorial-safe sightlines, salvage yards, and the first safehouse network.
2. **The Badlands** — rough ground, quarry roads, dust, steep risk/reward shortcuts, heavier obstacles, and long Fuel decisions.
3. **The Blackwood** — narrow forest roads, rain/night pressure, hidden service tracks, dense threat pockets, and late-game route mastery.

Names are working names until art/narrative validation. The functional contrast is required.

The region graph is physically connected but staged by visible repairable barriers and escalating conditions. Restoration unlocks crossings, services, return points, and shortcuts; it does not teleport the player into isolated level instances.

## Core gameplay loops

### First 30 seconds of controllable play

- Resume or start at the Garage without an account wall.
- A selected control layout is visible through concise, dismissible prompts.
- The immediate destination and return condition are clear.
- The player begins moving within ten seconds after drive control becomes available; first-launch language/accessibility setup is timed separately.
- A route fork, landmark silhouette, or tempting salvage signal is visible before the first minute.

### First five minutes

- Learn acceleration, steering, braking/reverse, and handbrake through use.
- Read road versus offroad value.
- Encounter one low-risk obstacle/threat and one optional reward.
- Reach the first destination or safehouse.
- Understand Fuel, Integrity, carried haul, and the option to continue or return.

### Typical 15-minute session

1. Choose a contract and loadout in under two minutes.
2. Drive through at least two meaningful route decisions.
3. Resolve one dynamic encounter or optional detour.
4. Complete, extract, or fail with a clear consequence.
5. Install, compare, sell, or save toward one part decision.

### Typical one-hour session

- Complete two or three expeditions.
- Discover a landmark, shortcut, blueprint, chassis clue, or safehouse improvement.
- Change or refine a build.
- Make visible progress toward a region restoration milestone.
- Finish at a clean autosave boundary.

### First day

- Complete onboarding and the Lowlands introduction.
- Install the first choice-bearing part during the first play session.
- Unlock the first safehouse and second activity family.
- Understand why a scout and rammer choose different routes.

### Current provisional-target first 6-10 active hours

- Restore access toward the D020-locked region set or approach the final region milestone; the current target is three region identities.
- Own the D020-locked chassis set and maintain multiple saved loadouts; the current target includes at least two chassis by this horizon.
- Complete the primary restoration arc or see its endpoint clearly.
- Begin mastery trials and region completion; if D020 retains Expedition Mode, it opens only after primary completion.

### Long-term and endgame

- World completion: landmarks, shortcuts, safehouses, blueprints, and mastery goals.
- Build mastery: improve times, damage efficiency, cargo recovery, and difficult route completion with different archetypes.
- If retained by D020, seeded Expedition Mode: the prototype's outward survival fantasy with production systems and fair deterministic seeds.
- Optional rotating challenge seeds only if offline fallback, no-loss participation, and privacy principles remain intact.
- Post-launch expansions may add regions, chassis, parts, encounter modules, and story arcs without invalidating launch saves or builds.

## Current provisional-target player journey

D020 may reduce region, chassis, or postgame quantity, but it preserves the complete ending and route/build/return progression.

1. **First launch** — language, display, audio, control preset, and high-value accessibility choices; then immediate Garage arrival.
2. **First vehicle** — the battered Utility wagon is already the player's usable car, not a menu-locked reward.
3. **Garage lot tutorial** — move, brake, reverse, drift, and hit a harmless breakable prop in a playable space.
4. **First expedition** — visible Lowlands safehouse objective; one fork and one optional salvage detour.
5. **First return and upgrade** — carried salvage is secured; the player chooses and installs one of two mutually useful parts, then can test/undo it in the lot.
6. **First meaningful destination** — restoring a safehouse changes navigation, return safety, and available contracts.
7. **First discovery** — a landmark or shortcut is marked through exploration, not an exhaustive icon dump.
8. **First specialization** — enough parts exist to commit toward scout, rammer, drifter, offroad, or hauler behavior.
9. **Midgame** — D020-locked region transitions introduce new terrain and route constraints; the current target uses a second chassis to broaden builds.
10. **Late game** — the D020-locked final-region or compact-finale pressure demands route knowledge, prepared loadouts, and selective risk; the current target uses Blackwood.
11. **Primary completion** — the county route network is restored and a final expedition demonstrates all core skills.
12. **Ongoing play** — unfinished discoveries, mastery trials, and alternate chassis/builds remain valuable without resetting the profile; Expedition Mode joins them only if retained by D020.

## Scope categories

`Required` means the capability must ship even when using it is optional for the player, such as accessibility assists. `Valuable` means the entire feature may be omitted from 1.0.

### Required for 1.0.0

- The complete title-independent identity, campaign ending, and D020-locked postgame/experience gates; post-completion Expedition Mode is the provisional target and first Required cut if capacity fails.
- Desktop-proven handling, hybrid world, activities, progression, content, UI, art, audio, save, settings, accessibility, and reliability.
- Interruption-safe active-expedition checkpoints. Restored-route fast travel and a small earned paint palette are provisional Required targets that D020 may reclassify before scale-up.
- Responsive mobile layouts and input built only after the corresponding desktop systems pass.
- Supported-device performance, lifecycle, interruption, offline, save, and TestFlight proof.
- App Store metadata, privacy, legal, accessibility, review, release, rollback, and support readiness.
- Paid base app with no launch IAP, ads, account, cross-app tracking, or mandatory backend.

### Valuable if capacity allows

- Additional chassis and sidegrade parts beyond the D020-locked Required target, only after required build diversity passes.
- Photo mode and shareable run card.
- Apple Game Center achievements and optional cloud-save synchronization.
- Alternate touch scheme such as tilt steering.
- Reversible fine tuning beyond part/loadout selection, expanded cosmetics, and optional featured Expedition seeds.
- Native iPad layout and its complete device/store-asset QA scope.
- Additional launch localization beyond the required localization-ready English build.
- More mastery trials, encounter variants, and cosmetic sets after the D020-locked content target and quality gates pass.

### Post-1.0 expansion

- New county region, route restoration arc, safehouses, and landmark set.
- Additional chassis families and part mechanics.
- Authored narrative expansion.
- Larger procedural Expedition rule sets.
- Asynchronous ghosts/leaderboards after privacy, cheating, and service-cost review.
- Multiplayer or co-op only as a separately funded architecture effort.
- Mod support only if platform and content-pipeline constraints justify it.
- A substantial paid expansion only after base 1.0 is complete and stable; never subscriptions, consumables, paid revives, or withheld base-game endings.

## Explicit non-goals

- No lane runner, corridor runner, or forced road-following.
- No realistic driving simulator, licensed cars, deep mechanical repair simulation, or manual transmission requirement.
- No on-foot mode, shooter controls, weapon inventory, base building, crafting-resource soup, or dialogue-heavy RPG.
- No pure infinite procedural world as the primary campaign.
- No MMO, real-time multiplayer, mandatory backend, account, or always-online dependency.
- No launch ads, loot boxes, energy, battle pass, subscriptions, or pay-to-win purchases.
- No feature is accepted because code exists; it must be integrated, playable, evidenced, documented, and inside performance/reliability budgets.
- No public title/logo/store identity is locked before formal clearance; execution assumes a rename unless written legal and positioning review affirmatively clears the exact title.

## Product success measures

- At least four of five new players drive within ten seconds and identify the immediate goal plus Fuel/Integrity within 30 seconds.
- Median handling satisfaction is at least 4/5 in structured desktop tests.
- At least three benchmarked builds remain viable across road, offroad, impact, cargo, and endurance tests; no one build wins every axis.
- Repeated 15-minute sessions contain a consequential route/resource/build/risk tradeoff at least every 30-45 seconds while driving; passive scenery, routine steering, and cosmetic pickups do not count.
- At least four of five blind target-player tests distinguish the promise as route-and-build restoration rather than describing it as the existing *Keep Driving*, “top-down *Pacific Drive*,” a rally game, or a generic zombie-driving game.
- Primary progression has no required grind wall longer than two normal sessions without a new capability, location, or meaningful part choice.
- Release beta records at least 1,000 launch-to-background-or-quit sessions across at least 50 external participants and every supported device tier over at least 14 consecutive days; at least 99.8% are free of attributable crash/watchdog/unrecovered hang, with zero reproducible save loss and zero open P0/P1.
- In at least ten clean-profile new-player campaign runs with active play timed separately from pause/background, median first primary-arc completion falls inside the D020-locked range (provisional target 8-12 hours; coherent fallback 5-8), at least eight finish inside the lock's declared tolerance, and none is blocked by mandatory random grind.
- At least five clean-profile mastery players each reach the D020-locked authored completion/build/mastery goal inside its active-play range (provisional target 20-30 hours; coherent fallback 10-15); median mastery enjoyment is >=4/5 and repeated identical filler is neither required nor counted. Expedition-specific goals apply only if Expedition Mode survives the scope lock.
- Supported iOS devices meet the declared frame, memory, thermal, battery, launch, resume, and input budgets.
